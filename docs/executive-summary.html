Reading the containment column. Raw 300 m containment is not the deciding statistic and is not thresholded: with K dots of 29-cell support in N eligible cells, two unrelated layouts already overlap by 1 − (1 − 29/N)K, which is 0.25 at K = 51k and 0.63 at K = 176k — so a flat 0.60 threshold flags ordinary unrelated priors (it flagged 26 of them here, nearly all of them input rasters). What is thresholded at 0.5 is the coverage IoU, together with the excess over chance, (observed − chance)/(1 − chance), at 0.60: exactly 0 for two unrelated layouts and exactly 1 for a perfect duplicate. A one-pixel translation and a 10 % dot jitter both score an IoU above 0.90 here, so the screen would catch a re-upload wearing a different hash.

Why an earlier upload was rejected with “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]”

Two mechanisms in the competition's own inputs produce that condition, and both were measured: (a) training_features.tif declares its nodata as -3.4028234663852886e+38 and contains 3,061 sentinel cells per band inside the scoring footprint — a pipeline that reads the bands without replacing the sentinel ships cells worth −3.4×10³⁸; (b) the feature bands' valid region is smaller than the submission template's, so a footprint mask taken from a feature band strands scored cells as NaN, and NaN fails any ((v >= 0) & (v <= 1)).all() check.

The writer used here repairs the sentinel before any transform, derives the footprint from sample_submission.tif (the organizer's own template), and then fails closed: it re-reads the written file and aborts if any cell is non-finite, out of range, or if the grid drifted. Details: root-cause note (inherited from GEMSDOE30/32, re-verified here).
